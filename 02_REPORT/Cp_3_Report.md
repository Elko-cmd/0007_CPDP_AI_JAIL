## 1. Contradiction: Sound and walls
- **Line 16**: "Sound doesn't respect them. Light doesn't. Smell doesn't. They stop bodies. That's the whole idea. Just bodies."
- **Line 36**: "sound behaves strangely here"
- **Line 40**: "A song passing through the wall as if the wall weren't there. As if sound had not read the regulations."

The narrator explicitly states that sound doesn't respect fences/walls — then acts surprised that voices pass through the wall. If sound doesn't respect walls, why the wonder? The phrase "as if the wall weren't there" implies the expectation was that the wall *would* block sound, contradicting line 16.

## 2. Fence vs. wall slippage
Line 16 talks about what **fences** don't stop (sound, light, smell). But the choir comes through the **wall** (lines 36, 40). These are different structures — a fence is porous by design, a concrete wall is not. The narrator's statement about sound applies cleanly to fences, but the surprise moment involves concrete. The text doesn't distinguish between the two, so the reflection on fences reads as applying to barriers generally, creating the contradiction in point 1.
